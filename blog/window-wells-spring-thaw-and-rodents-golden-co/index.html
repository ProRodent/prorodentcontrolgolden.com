Window Wells, Spring Thaw and Rodents in Golden

When snow melts, ground that was frozen turns soft and wet. Here is what the thaw does to rodent behavior, why window wells matter and what to check in the first warm weeks.

Winter ends in stages along the Front Range. A warm day melts snow, a cold night refreezes it and the ground turns soggy. It is a messy season, and it affects rodents as much as it affects people.

This guide covers the first warm weeks after snow, with a focus on one feature: the window well. It also lists what to check in a basement and around the foundation.

What the thaw changes.

Frozen ground thaws from the top down, and meltwater collects against foundations and in low spots. Burrows can flood, and animals that dug into the soil may seek drier places. A garage, a shed or a basement can fit that description.

The thaw also shows what winter did. Cracks widen as water freezes and expands, and sealant pulls away. A gap that was fine in the fall may be open now.

Window wells and why they matter.

A window well is a small pit around a basement window. Leaves, soil and trash collect in it, and snow melts into it. For a mouse or a rat, a well offers cover, and a gap at the frame offers a way in.

A good cover helps in two ways. It keeps debris out and removes cover. Make sure it fits well and leaves no gap at the edges.

A first-warm-weeks checklist.

  • Circle the house and look into each well and at each foundation vent.
  • Check where pipes, cables and gas lines enter the basement.
  • Look for droppings on the basement floor, near the water heater and behind stored boxes.
  • Sniff the basement for a sour or musty odor.
  • Examine the garage door seal and the slab edge.

Photograph each spot and make a list. A short list of repairs is easy to tackle, and the pictures help if you phone for help.

Closing gaps that winter opened.

Use metal. Hardware cloth, flashing and hard-cure compound hold up against teeth, while foam and caulk alone do not. See the exclusion page for materials and the garage and basement page for the spots that matter most.

If you are unsure whether a gap matters, use a quarter inch as the threshold and close anything wider.

Cleaning a basement safely.

A basement shut up for the winter can have dust and droppings in the corners. Open windows and doors, step away for about thirty minutes, then moisten the droppings with disinfectant ahead of any wiping. The steps are in the safety box below.

If you see mold or standing water, treat that as a separate problem. We deal with rodents and will say when something else needs another trade.

When to phone.

  • You see droppings or hear sounds in the walls.
  • You find burrows near the foundation.
  • A smell appears after the thaw.
  • You notice chewed insulation, pipe wrap or stored items.

An inspection shows where animals enter. Trapping and sealing follow, in that order.

Landscape changes worth making.

Spring is also the time to fix the ground around the house. Pull soil and mulch back from the foundation, rake leaves out of the wells and make sure downspouts carry water well away from the walls. Wet soil beside the house keeps burrowing animals interested.

Drainage work belongs to other trades, but a few simple habits cost nothing. Keep a hand's width of bare ground at the base of the wall, and look at the same spots each April.

Dust and droppings: ground rules first

The CDC steps appear here in plain words so you need not hunt for them.

  • Skip brooms and vacuums entirely. They throw dry droppings into the air you breathe, so they stay in the closet.
  • Open what you can, walk outside and wait roughly thirty minutes while stale air clears.
  • Disposable gloves of rubber, vinyl or latex belong on your hands for anything soiled, and a thorough soap-and-water wash belongs at the end.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Flu-like symptoms after contact with rodent waste are a medical matter, so tell your physician about the exposure.

A few more answers.

Why do window wells matter?

They collect leaves and debris, and animals can shelter there or find gaps at the frame. A cover and a clean-out help.

Does spring thaw send rodents indoors?

It can push burrowing animals toward dry shelter, and gaps that opened during winter may let them in.

Should I check the basement in spring?

Yes. Look for droppings, damp spots and gaps around pipes and windows.

What makes a good window well cover?

A clear, sturdy cover that fits snugly and lets in light. Check that it leaves no gaps at the edges.

Can you help with drainage?

We deal with rodents. Drainage and grading belong to other trades, and we will say so if it matters.
