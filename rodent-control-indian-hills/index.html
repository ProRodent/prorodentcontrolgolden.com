Why setting matters.

We will not guess how any street in Indian Hills looks. We ask whether your lot is wooded or open, whether a deck or crawl space sits under the house and whether wood, feed or stored gear leans on an outside wall.

Plantings and piles shape rodent behavior more than owners expect. A branch resting on the roof becomes a bridge, dense shrubs against the foundation hide the wall, and stacked wood gives cover. One unhurried lap outside shows which of these fit your address.

Wood, feed and stored gear.

Firewood stacks, bagged feed and boxes of stored gear are the three things rodents find most useful outside a house. If any lean on the wall, shift them a few feet away and lift them off the ground.

Cabins and spare buildings that stand closed for weeks need extra care. Air them out before you step in, and read the wet-cleaning steps in the safety box below before you touch droppings.

Found droppings? Begin here

Read these six points before touching a pellet, nest or stain. They come from the CDC and not from our opinion.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Before you step into a cabin, shed, attic or garage that has been shut for ages, let fresh air flow through for thirty minutes.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Illness after a cleanup belongs with a doctor and not an exterminator. Describe the job to a physician.
