Why the garage gets chosen first.

The garage tends to be the least tended part of a house. The door seal wears, boxes lean against the walls and a side door sits a little ajar. To a mouse that means warmth, dryness and nest material.

In Colorado a garage often holds both winter and summer gear: skis, camp stoves, coolers, bikes and bins. Much of it is soft or hollow, and much of it stays untouched for months. That suits rodents and hurts you.

Garage checklist.

  • The bottom seal and each corner of the main door, hunting for daylight or chewed rubber.
  • The line between wall and slab, where cracks open as the ground shifts.
  • Shelving pressed to the wall, which hides a rodent lane.
  • Pet food, birdseed and fertilizer sacks, plus paper or fabric kept low.
  • Holes for wires and pipes, and the shared wall with the house.

A technician usually spends the first stretch on the door and the slab edge. Those two spots explain many garage entries, and they are the quickest to improve.

Basements and window wells.

Basements offer other doorways. Foundation cracks, pipe sleeves, dryer vents and window wells can each let animals in, and the dim corners of a storage room make a fine nest site.

Keeping the room dull to mice.

After the gaps are shut and the animals gone, a few habits help. Leave a hand's width between stored items and the wall so droppings stay visible. Swap cardboard for lidded plastic. Keep seed, pet food and fertilizer in sealed bins, and sweep crumbs away.

Cleaning a garage that sat shut.

A garage closed for weeks may hold dried droppings in its dust. Open the doors, step away for roughly thirty minutes and leave the broom alone. Moisten droppings with disinfectant ahead of wiping, and work through the CDC steps in the safety box below.

Deer mice favor sheds and cabins, though they can appear in garages near open land. That is one more reason to use the wet method every single time.

How many visits.

Many garage jobs need one visit for inspection and trapping and a second for sealing. When a basement hides a bigger problem, the technician splits it into stages and says which comes first.

Do these while the day is open.

  • Clear a path to the attic hatch or the crawl space door.
  • Put away loose pet food and birdseed overnight.
  • Show the technician any trap or bait you used, so nothing is missed.
  • Stay clear of wiring that looks chewed or bare.

Keep the dust down with this short list

Start with this list, then decide which tasks are yours and which to hand off.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Disposable gloves of rubber, vinyl or latex belong on your hands for anything soiled, and a thorough soap-and-water wash belongs at the end.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Illness after a cleanup belongs with a doctor and not an exterminator. Describe the job to a physician.

Garage and basement: what people ask.

What do mice see in a garage?

Shelter, quiet, stored paper and fabric for bedding, and sometimes pet kibble or seed. A tired door seal supplies the entrance.

Can a window well admit animals?

It can. Wells fill with leaves and debris, and animals that fall in or shelter there may find a gap at the frame. A cover and a clean-out help.

Does cardboard matter that much?

Cardboard shreds easily into bedding. Shifting valuables into lidded plastic bins takes that away.

What about the second fridge or freezer?

Look at the floor around it. Warm compressors and stored food draw rodents, so keep that corner clean and tight.

How about gear in a ski box or camper?

Open and inspect it before the season. Chewed fabric or droppings mean the gear needs cleaning as the safety box describes.
