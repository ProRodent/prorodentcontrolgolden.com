Trouble that starts at the lot line.

Norway rats live low to the ground. They dig by foundations, beneath slabs and sheds and along fences, and they use the same trails every night. For that reason the visit starts outside and moves to the garage, crawl space or attic afterward.

A hillside lot offers rock edges, ground cover, wood stacks and open slope. A flat in-town lot offers trash areas, alley corners and gaps beside old plumbing. Either can host rats, and neither has to be dirty.

Signs worth mentioning on the call.

  • Golf-ball-sized holes next to a foundation, shed or fence post, often with smooth worn rims.
  • Thick pellets along a wall, a rail or under a deck.
  • Greasy smudges where a body brushes the same corner nightly.
  • Chewed edges on wood, plastic pipe or the foot of a door.
  • Seed, pet kibble or fruit that is gone by morning.

No single sign proves rats. Several together, plus sounds after dark, give the technician a picture to test.

Trapping, then cutting the food.

Rats are wary of anything new, so traps may stay unbaited or lightly baited for a few days before being set. The delay is intentional. A rat that springs a trap and survives teaches its neighbors to steer clear.

Reducing supply counts equally. Bring seed inside, cover compost, take pet bowls in at night and raise wood stacks onto a rack. The backyard and outdoor page shows which changes pay off first.

Shutting out the next one.

When trapping slows, exclusion work closes the openings. Rats chew soft seals, so foam and plastic are wasted on them. Steel mesh, flashing and hard-cure sealant hold where softer products fail.

Some rats tunnel under slabs and footings, and that cannot be sealed from inside. In such cases the plan may include mesh buried along the foundation edge, which we explain during the walk-through.

The first week.

Expect a visit that spends as long outside as inside, then a return within days to empty traps and test the repairs. If rats keep turning up, the technician looks for what was missed, such as a neighbor's feeder or a shed door left unlatched.

Rats leave droppings and urine where you may later want to clean. Read the safety box below before touching anything, and book attic and crawl space cleanup if the mess spreads beyond a corner.

Steps for today.

  • If you must enter a dusty space, wear a mask rated for fine particles.
  • Keep fans off in rooms with droppings, since moving air lifts dust.
  • Leave closets and cabinets with gnawing untouched until the inspection.
  • List what the animals damaged, with photos.

Keep the dust down with this short list

A few minutes of reading now keeps dust out of the air later. The points follow the agency's own sequence.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Disposable gloves of rubber, vinyl or latex belong on your hands for anything soiled, and a thorough soap-and-water wash belongs at the end.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Illness after a cleanup belongs with a doctor and not an exterminator. Describe the job to a physician.

Rat questions we hear in Golden.

How big an opening do rats need?

About a thumb's width, a little under an inch. Rats chew to enlarge a gap, so anything they can push their nose into deserves a closer look.

Do creeks bring rats?

Water, cover and food along a creek suit Norway rats generally. We hold no counts for Golden itself, so we begin with your lot and let the signs speak.

Will a rat go away unaided?

Seldom, while shelter and food remain. Taking away the outdoor draw matters as much as trapping.

Is rat bait wise?

We urge caution. Bait can poison pets and wildlife and can leave a carcass in a wall. The EPA page in the sources box explains why products face limits.

Do you work on restaurants and shops?

Yes. Commercial jobs start at the delivery door and the dumpster. The commercial page walks through the routine.
