Why the order matters.

Cleanup closes a rodent job, it does not open one. If animals still use the attic, fresh droppings arrive faster than you can clear the old ones. The usual sequence is inspection, trapping, sealing and then cleanup.

Once cleanup starts, the aim is to remove what is contaminated while keeping dust out of the air. That is why public health guidance calls for wet methods and not brooms.

Steps inside the space.

  • Airing: doors and hatches stay open about half an hour before work begins.
  • Soaking: disinfectant goes onto droppings and nests, which then sit for several minutes.
  • Removal: wet material goes into sealed bags, along with any dead animals.
  • Wiping: surfaces are cleaned and treated, and tools are disinfected.
  • Replacing: new insulation goes in only where needed.

The technician wears a respirator, gloves and disposable coveralls, and bags leave by a route that avoids tracking waste through the house.

Insulation, ducts and what can stay.

Loose fiberglass and cellulose soak up urine and trap droppings, so heavily soiled patches usually come out. Lightly touched batts can sometimes be treated and kept. The technician shows photos of what was found.

Crawl spaces under foothill homes.

Many homes on sloped lots have a crawl space or partial basement, and rodents like them because they are dark and undisturbed. We enter when the space is safe and dry, and we tell you if standing water, loose wiring or low clearance makes entry unwise.

Vapor barriers, pipe wrap and under-floor insulation often need attention after rodent trouble. Each is priced separately so you choose which to do.

A word on health.

The CDC links rodent waste to hantavirus and other illnesses, and dust is how the risk spreads. If you decide to enter an attic yourself, read the safety box below first. Never sweep, never vacuum and never skip the wait after wetting.

If you feel feverish or short of breath in the weeks afterward, see a doctor and mention the cleanup. We cannot give medical advice, and the sooner a physician knows, the better.

After the cleanup.

With the space clean and dry, a last look for gnawing at the repairs is wise. With entries sound and activity ended, the attic or crawl space is ready for new insulation or damage repair if needed.

Interim precautions.

  • If you must enter a dusty space, wear a mask rated for fine particles.
  • Keep fans off in rooms with droppings, since moving air lifts dust.
  • Leave closets and cabinets with gnawing untouched until the inspection.
  • List what the animals damaged, with photos.

Safe cleanup in six brief points

Whether you take on the mess yourself or hand it to the technician, this order keeps particles out of your lungs.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Open what you can, walk outside and wait roughly thirty minutes while stale air clears.
  • Hands carry waste to faces, so gloves go on first and a careful wash comes last.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Collect the soaked material on paper towels, seal it in a bag and throw it out with the household garbage.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.

Cleanup: what people ask.

May I do it myself?

You can when the area is small and you follow the CDC steps: ventilate, glove up, soak, wipe and bag. Large attic jobs are dusty and better left to people with the equipment.

Is new insulation part of the job?

It can be, once animals are gone and entries are shut. Replacement is priced as its own line.

Why wait until after trapping and sealing?

New animals foul a clean space again within days, so cleaning before the entries are shut wastes money.

How can I tell if insulation needs replacing?

Flattened, stained, clumped or smelly insulation is a candidate. The technician checks with a light and explains what is worth keeping.

Will the smell fade?

A lingering smell usually fades once the source is gone and the space is treated and aired. A dead animal needs separate handling.
