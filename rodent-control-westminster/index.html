Open ground, fences and drainage lines.

Where a lot backs onto open ground, a path or a drainage line, rodents have a ready-made route. Say so on the call, and the technician starts at that edge of the property.

A fence that meets the house works like a ladder, and a fence with a gap beneath works like a door. Both are easy to spot in daylight.

Keep the dust down with this short list

A few minutes of reading now keeps dust out of the air later. The points follow the agency's own sequence.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Disposable gloves of rubber, vinyl or latex belong on your hands for anything soiled, and a thorough soap-and-water wash belongs at the end.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Flu-like symptoms after contact with rodent waste are a medical matter, so tell your physician about the exposure.
