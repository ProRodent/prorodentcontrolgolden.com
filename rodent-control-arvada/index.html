Holding steps.

  • If you must enter a dusty space, wear a mask rated for fine particles.
  • Keep fans off in rooms with droppings, since moving air lifts dust.
  • Leave closets and cabinets with gnawing untouched until the inspection.
  • List what the animals damaged, with photos.

Handling the mess the CDC way

Read these six points before touching a pellet, nest or stain. They come from the CDC and not from our opinion.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Flu-like symptoms after contact with rodent waste are a medical matter, so tell your physician about the exposure.
