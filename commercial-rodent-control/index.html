A record you can show.

Businesses need more than a quick fix. A manager, landlord or insurer may ask what happened and when. Every visit therefore ends with a written note on what was checked, what turned up and what we did.

The routine matches residential work: look, trap, seal and return. What changes is the schedule and the paperwork.

First stops.

  • The delivery door, including its threshold and the gaps at the corners.
  • Dumpster pads, grease bins and recycling areas.
  • Storerooms, dry goods shelving and walk-in coolers.
  • Kitchens: under equipment, behind the line and around floor drains.
  • Ceiling voids, utility chases and shared walls.
  • Exterior walls, roof edges and any plants touching the building.

In a food business the questions start with what rodents can eat and where they can hide. Cleaning and storage habits count as much as traps.

Scheduling and access.

We schedule around your hours. Some businesses prefer visits before opening, others after closing. Tell the dispatcher who holds the keys, whether an alarm is set and which door to use.

A single call is welcome too. If you need only one visit to deal with a problem, we handle it and explain what to do afterward.

Habits your staff can keep.

  • Close trash lids and empty bins at day's end.
  • Store dry goods in sealed containers off the floor.
  • Keep doors shut except during deliveries.
  • Report droppings, gnawing or noises to the manager at once.

For what happens after the first visit, see trapping and removal and inspection and estimates.

Between the call and the arrival.

  • Shut the worst room and switch off any fan that pushes air through it.
  • Never move a nest or nesting material with bare hands.
  • Leave a suspect vent alone for now, because blocking it can trap an animal inside.
  • Keep a short dated log for the technician.

Do and do not, for rodent waste

Dull but important: six reminders for anyone about to handle rodent waste.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Before you step into a cabin, shed, attic or garage that has been shut for ages, let fresh air flow through for thirty minutes.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.

Yes, within the ZIP codes we cover. Kitchens and food storage get priority checks.

No. It is rodent inspection and control. Health code questions go to the proper agency.
