The name, the peak and the limits of what we know.

Lookout Mountain is a peak about 1.7 miles west-southwest of downtown Golden, 7,377 feet high, in an unincorporated part of Jefferson County. Buffalo Bill is buried there, and the mountain also has a museum. We give no opening year, because we could not verify one. What matters for a rodent call up there is the house, the crawl space and the road in.

People use the mountain's name for the homes and roads around it, and no surveyed boundary came with the name. We therefore make no claims about streets, neighbors or building ages. If you live in the area, give your street address and ZIP code and the dispatcher will confirm.

What mountain homes tend to share.

We cannot describe any particular house, but some features are common to sloped, wooded lots in general. They are worth checking on your own property, whatever its size or age.

  • A deck or porch on posts, with a dark space beneath.
  • A crawl space or partial basement on the downhill side.
  • Firewood stacked beside the house or under the deck.
  • Rock, scrub and open ground close to the foundation.
  • A shed, garage or outbuilding set back from the house.

Each of these can offer shelter, and each can be improved in an afternoon.

Closed for the week, open to deer mice.

Many mountain homes sit empty for days or weeks, and a quiet building draws deer mice. The CDC links deer mice to hantavirus, so cleanup in a shut-up cabin should always be wet. Air the building for about half an hour first, then soak droppings and wipe them up.

The CDC prevention page, linked in the sources box, describes the steps in plain language. Read it before opening a long-closed shed or crawl space.

Trapping and sealing on a slope.

Trapping removes the animals inside the building, and sealing keeps new ones out. On a mountain lot, sealing may include mesh along the deck skirt, closures at crawl space vents and fresh seals on the garage door.

If your property has access challenges, such as a steep drive or winter snow, mention them when you call. The dispatcher plans around them.

The seasonal rhythm.

  • Late summer: close gaps and clear stored items before the nights turn cold.
  • Autumn: stack firewood away from walls and check door seals.
  • Winter: listen at night and photograph any droppings you find.
  • Spring: look for new gaps after the thaw and clear the deck skirt.

A simple dated log helps the technician work out where and when the animals got in.

How we handle the first visit.

Outdoors comes first, with a lap around the building, then the crawl space, attic and any garage. Traps follow the evidence, and the entries are sealed once activity ends. A written plan arrives with the quote, and a return trip checks the work.

On your side of the door.

  • Quiet the room, then listen from one spot and note where the sound seems strongest.
  • Skip foggers and sprays that only drive animals deeper into the structure.
  • Check that the garage door seal lies flat against the slab.
  • Photograph anything in daylight that resembles an entry gap.

Handling the mess the CDC way

A few minutes of reading now keeps dust out of the air later. The points follow the agency's own sequence.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Before you step into a cabin, shed, attic or garage that has been shut for ages, let fresh air flow through for thirty minutes.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Soak the mess through and leave it around five minutes, so the dust cannot rise.
  • Collect the soaked material on paper towels, seal it in a bag and throw it out with the household garbage.
  • Flu-like symptoms after contact with rodent waste are a medical matter, so tell your physician about the exposure.

About this page.

Is Lookout Mountain inside Golden?

Wikipedia describes the peak and its park as part of an unincorporated area of Jefferson County, and places the peak about 1.7 miles west-southwest of downtown Golden. We call this the mountain area west of Golden.

Will you drive up the mountain?

We confirm by street address and ZIP code. 80419 is one of our home ZIP codes. We cannot say which codes mountain addresses use, so give your street and the ZIP on your mail.

Are cabins a bigger risk?
