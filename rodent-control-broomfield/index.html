Freeze-thaw and your walls.

Winter brings the most sightings indoors, but spring thaw matters too. Melting snow soaks the ground and can send burrowing animals searching for dry shelter. Check window wells and the base of walls once the thaw ends.

Garages, fences and the lot edge.

Two quiet features of many lots are the garage and the back fence. Check the garage door seal, and look at where the fence meets the house, because both are spots where animals get in.

If a field or drainage line runs behind the lot, say so on the call. Open ground gives rodents cover close to the building, and the technician begins on that side.

The visit follows a fixed order. We inspect and map the routes, set traps along them and then close gaps with steel and sealant. Sealing follows trapping so no animal is shut inside a wall. Cleanup and repair are saved for after the animals have left and every gap is shut.

Sounds, odors and leftovers.

Rodent evidence is easy to overlook until you know the signs. Hold this list up against your property.

  • Rattling in the attic on cold nights.
  • Chewed corners on cereal boxes or bread bags.
  • Shredded-paper nests behind stored items.
  • A gap under the garage door that lets in light.
  • A dead-animal smell that strengthens by the day.

Safe cleanup in six brief points

Hurrying a cleanup can do more harm than the mess. Keep to the order shown.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Before you step into a cabin, shed, attic or garage that has been shut for ages, let fresh air flow through for thirty minutes.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Flu-like symptoms after contact with rodent waste are a medical matter, so tell your physician about the exposure.
