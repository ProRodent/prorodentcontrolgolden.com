A lap around the house.

  • Stand at each corner and sight along the foundation for gaps, loose vent covers and holes at the base of the siding.
  • Check where pipes, cables and gas lines come in.
  • Press on the garage door seal and look for daylight under the door.
  • Look up at the roofline for gaps at the fascia, soffit and chimney flashing.
  • Note any branch, trellis or step that touches the house.

Ten slow minutes often reveal the first suspect gap. Take a photo of whatever you see.

Weather and timing.

Cold sends animals looking for warmth. Freezing and thawing can shift masonry and siding, so a gap that was tight in September may open by midwinter. Walk the house after the first hard freeze and once more in spring.

What a visit looks like.

The sequence runs outside first, then attic, basement or crawl space, then garage. The technician follows droppings and grease marks, sets traps on the routes and, after the animals are gone, closes each opening with metal. A written plan lists every step and price, and a return visit checks the results.

Safety while you wait.

Never sweep or vacuum droppings. Should you clean, follow the wet method in the safety box below, keep kids and pets out of the affected room, and skip the bait.

Do these while the day is open.

  • Seal up food tonight, pet kibble and seed included.
  • Let any trap you already placed stay exactly where it sits.
  • Keep bare hands out of the dark corners of an attic or crawl space.
  • Notice which outside door or window gets used most after sunset.

Safe cleanup in six brief points

Everything below restates federal public health advice, so it applies whether or not you ever book a visit.

  • Skip brooms and vacuums entirely. They throw dry droppings into the air you breathe, so they stay in the closet.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Hands carry waste to faces, so gloves go on first and a careful wash comes last.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.
