What draws a mouse to one building.

A mouse looks for three things close together: a dry place to sleep, something edible within a few feet and a protected path between them. The cavity beside a pantry ticks every box. A stack of plastic bins near the garage door or a loose batt by the attic hatch can do the same.

Along the Front Range the mouse season is long. Falling temperatures send animals toward heated buildings, and repeated freezing and thawing can open hairline cracks where siding, foundation and sill plate meet. Neither factor requires a messy household.

Mice stay busy mostly after dark, so many people notice them only once the house falls silent. Spend a minute listening beside the pantry wall, the water heater closet and any garage wall shared with the kitchen.

Follow the trail before setting anything.

The inspection starts from the newest sign and spreads outward. Fresh pellets, greasy marks along baseboards and a cereal box with chewed corners together sketch a rough map, and that map always ends at a gap. Traps set without it catch a few mice while the hole stays wide open.

  • Cabinets beneath kitchen and bath sinks, the gap behind the stove and fridge, and the water heater closet.
  • In the garage, at each bottom corner of the door, along its rubber seal and where the wall meets the slab.
  • On the roof side, around the attic hatch, vents and any spot where a line passes through.
  • Around the base, at window wells, crawl space vents and skirting on the shaded side.

Trap, then seal.

Snap traps or enclosed stations follow the evidence trail and sit beyond the reach of kids and pets. Mice hug walls and poke at anything unfamiliar, so a trap set right at the baseboard does better than one out on open floor.

Sealing starts when the first catches confirm which routes are active. Steel wool by itself gets dragged out by determined teeth, so each opening receives flashing, mesh and hard-cure sealant. The exclusion page explains our choice of metals.

Cabins, sheds and deer mice.

Deer mice act much like house mice, and they favor sheds, cabins and detached garages that stand closed for weeks. The CDC ties deer mice to hantavirus, so do not sweep droppings in a shut-up building. Read the wet-cleaning steps in the safety box before you tidy.

Close any outbuilding opening before you clean. Cleanup raises a little dust, so the fewer return trips the mice make, the fewer times you have to do it.

Signs that it worked.

Success sounds like silence: no new pellets, and traps that remain empty. Heavily soiled wall voids may need attic and crawl space cleanup, and chewed wiring or insulation belongs under rodent damage repair.

Can you tell if the noise comes from mice or from something bigger? An inspection names the animal and hands you a plan.

Interim precautions.

  • Close the door on the noisiest room and leave it shut until the technician comes.
  • Hold off on sweeping or vacuuming pellets, since both fling dry particles upward.
  • Pass on loose bait, because a poisoned animal can crawl into a wall and die there.
  • Jot down the hour of each noise so a pattern shows.

Cleanup in the right order

Start with this list, then decide which tasks are yours and which to hand off.

  • Skip brooms and vacuums entirely. They throw dry droppings into the air you breathe, so they stay in the closet.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.

Mouse questions from Golden callers.

What size gap lets a mouse through?

Roughly the thickness of a pencil. Look beside drain lines, behind dryer ducts and around door frames, plus any place a cable pokes through siding.

Is it only a winter problem?

No. Cold weather sends many animals inside, but a heated house pulls them in all year, and a crack from spring can be the summer entrance.

May I put out bait from the hardware store?

Check with the technician beforehand. Poisoned mice frequently die inside a wall cavity, and the resulting odor is harder to manage than the mice were.

How soon will the scratching stop?

The answer turns on how many mice are inside and how many entrances they use. A realistic timeline arrives after the walk-through and never ahead of it.

Yes. Overnight, a live person picks up, and if the day has room a same-day visit is offered.
