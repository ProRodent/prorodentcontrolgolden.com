How a Golden property reads to a rodent.

Clear Creek leaves the mountains at Golden and runs along the base of the Front Range, with Lookout Mountain on one side and the two Table Mountains on the other. Creek banks, open slopes and tidy residential blocks sit within minutes of each other.

Whether rodents live nearby is rarely the interesting question, since with this much cover some surely do. The better question is whether your building hands them a way in. Twenty minutes outside with a flashlight tells you more than listening from the kitchen.

Naming the animal.

A house mouse passes through a gap about as thick as a pencil. Deer mice do likewise and lean toward sheds, cabins and outbuildings. Norway rats want an opening nearer the width of a thumb, and they dig along foundations, slab edges and creek banks.

Species decides the trap, the metal and the cleanup, so an identification precedes any quote. Your description over the phone narrows it down, and the walk-through settles it.

Hard cold and the gaps it exposes.

When temperatures fall, outdoor animals drift toward warmth, and a heated house with a tired door sweep looks like a bargain. Freezing and thawing can also widen cracks where siding, masonry and slab meet, so a gap that looked tight in September may stand open by February.

  • Before the first freeze, wiggle each vent cover and pipe collar and crouch to check for daylight beneath the garage door.
  • Move birdseed, pet food and grass seed into lidded tubs.
  • Stack firewood on a rack, well away from the siding.
  • Trim any limb that reaches the roof, the gutter or a wall.

Until the technician arrives.

Three habits keep everyone safe and preserve the evidence. Do not disturb droppings. Steer kids and pets clear of the affected room. Let any trap already set stay put.

  • Photograph droppings and bite marks with a coin beside them for scale.
  • Record the room and the time each time something moves.
  • Shut the door of the busiest room.
  • Skip loose bait and sticky boards, each of which creates problems of its own.

What a visit looks like.

Whether it is one pantry mouse or a family in a hillside crawl space, the job moves through the same four stages.

  1. 1

    Look.

    The walk-through begins at the strongest evidence and then circles the roofline, foundation and yard, noting fresh droppings and the holes in use.

  2. 2

    Trap.

    Traps or stations go onto the lanes the signs reveal, chosen for the species and placed where kids and pets cannot get at them.

  3. 3

    Seal.

    Flashing, steel mesh or a hard-cure sealant closes each gap, vent and gnawed opening against future teeth.

  4. 4

    Return.

    A second visit empties traps, tests every repair and picks up any cleanup or damage work still waiting.

Signs that something already lives in the building.

When two or three of these gather in one corner, the activity is probably current and not left over from last year.

  • Droppings.

    Dark grains under a sink, along a baseboard, across a garage shelf or on top of attic insulation.

  • New gnawing.

    Fresh bite marks look pale and ragged on a rafter, a plastic pipe, a wire jacket or a cereal box.

  • Night noise.

    Scratching, light patter or a muffled knock from inside a wall or above a ceiling after the house goes quiet.

  • Nest scraps.

    A wad of shredded paper, tugged insulation, fabric bits or dry grass wedged into a sheltered corner.

  • Rub marks.

    Dark smears on walls, pipes and joists trace the path an animal walks each night.

  • Odd smells.

    A sharp ammonia tang in a shut room, or a heavy sweetness where something died out of sight.

Two or three of those sound familiar? Phone before the damage spreads.

Landmarks callers use to place themselves.

Colorado School of Mines.

The Colorado School of Mines was founded in 1874 and borders Clear Creek. Some callers mention it to say roughly where they live. Please add your street too, because the campus says nothing about your roofline, your garage door or the gap behind your dryer.

The Coors brewery.

Coors began in Golden in 1873, and Wikipedia describes the Golden brewery as the largest single-site brewery in the world. We know nothing about how any nearby business manages pests. If you run a food or drink operation anywhere in town, tell us about the delivery door and dumpster, since an inspection of your building starts there.

Colorado Railroad Museum.

The Colorado Railroad Museum, at 17155 W 44th Avenue, opened in 1959 and holds more than 100 locomotives and cars across about 19.5 acres, along Clear Creek. Creek corridors are one reason we ask about wood stacks, sheds and ground cover during an inspection, so tell us what leans on your walls.

Buffalo Bill Museum and Grave.

Buffalo Bill is buried on Lookout Mountain, which sits just outside the city in unincorporated Jefferson County. Mentioning the museum is a handy way to say you live up the mountain. Give the street address too, because a mountain home calls for a different first inspection: crawl space, deck posts and firewood.

Why the neighborhood changes where we start.

A landmark helps us picture the area, but it never reveals what is nesting inside your walls. A quiet cul-de-sac, a lot backing onto open slope and a restaurant beside a loading dock would each send the inspection somewhere different.

Think about vehicles too. A truck, camper, trailer or ski-season car left alone for weeks can shelter a nest, so open the hood and smell the cabin vents when you get back. A chewed wire caught early costs much less than a new harness.

Buildings that sit empty.

Many Front Range houses and mountain cabins stand unused for weeks, and a quiet building tempts rodents. Before leaving, clear open food from the pantry, close the gap beneath the garage door and ask somebody to look in occasionally. When you return, peek at the attic hatch before unpacking.

A territorial capital with ordinary walls.

Golden served as a territorial capital in the 1860s, and many of its buildings are still in daily use. Age is only a rough clue for rodent work, because what we examine are the real gaps, vents and pipe entries on your house, shop or garage, and one phone call is enough to start that.

First questions, short replies.

Is anyone there at night?

Yes, overnight and on holidays. A visit the same day is possible when the schedule has a gap, and if not, you hear the next open slot.

How do I tell a mouse from a rat?

Size and location give hints. Rice-sized pellets suggest mice, while larger blunt ones suggest rats. Heavy footfalls overhead at dusk, or a burrow beside the foundation, also point to rats. We confirm on site.

Should I try poison first?

We recommend against it. A rodent that eats bait frequently dies inside a wall, and that smell is harder to fix than the original problem. For the product rules, read the EPA page linked below.
