Dust and droppings: ground rules first

Read these six points before touching a pellet, nest or stain. They come from the CDC and not from our opinion.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Before you step into a cabin, shed, attic or garage that has been shut for ages, let fresh air flow through for thirty minutes.
  • Disposable gloves of rubber, vinyl or latex belong on your hands for anything soiled, and a thorough soap-and-water wash belongs at the end.
  • Drench droppings and nests, and the surface under them, with disinfectant or a bleach mix. Let it work for about five minutes.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Illness after a cleanup belongs with a doctor and not an exterminator. Describe the job to a physician.

Where this advice originates

Each safety point here traces to published agency guidance, not to anyone selling a service.

  • EPA listing of rodenticide restrictions: What the restrictions imply for anyone thinking of poison as a first step.
  • CDC guidance on rodent dropping cleanup: Gloves to wear, how long disinfectant needs to sit and the proper way to dispose of bagged waste.
