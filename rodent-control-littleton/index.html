Weather and rodent behavior.

Winter brings the most sightings indoors, but spring thaw matters too. Melting snow soaks the ground and can send burrowing animals searching for dry shelter. Check window wells and the base of walls once the thaw ends.

Evidence that earns a visit.

Rodent evidence is easy to overlook until you know the signs. Hold this list up against your property.

  • Soft scampering in the ceiling at dusk.
  • Pellets scattered over a shelf or workbench.
  • Insulation pulled loose or flattened.
  • Birdseed that disappears overnight.
  • A stain on drywall with a sour odor.

Attached garages and everyday clutter.

An attached garage joins the house to the outdoors. If a mouse gets into the garage, the door into the house is the next barrier, so its seal and the gap beneath it deserve a close look.

Boxes and bins stacked on the garage wall give animals cover. Pull them out a few inches so droppings show, and trade cardboard for lidded plastic when you can.

Before the visit.

  • Quiet the room, then listen from one spot and note where the sound seems strongest.
  • Skip foggers and sprays that only drive animals deeper into the structure.
  • Check that the garage door seal lies flat against the slab.
  • Photograph anything in daylight that resembles an entry gap.

Before anyone picks up a mop

Whether you take on the mess yourself or hand it to the technician, this order keeps particles out of your lungs.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Open what you can, walk outside and wait roughly thirty minutes while stale air clears.
  • Wear disposable gloves throughout and wash with soap and running water when they come off.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Illness after a cleanup belongs with a doctor and not an exterminator. Describe the job to a physician.
