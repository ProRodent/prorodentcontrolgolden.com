Seasons and entry points.

Cold snaps push animals toward warm buildings, and freeze and thaw cycles can open hairline cracks around doors, vents and foundations. A walk around the building after the first hard freeze, and again in spring, catches most new gaps.

  • Skip foggers and sprays that only drive animals deeper into the structure.

Dust and droppings: ground rules first

Start with this list, then decide which tasks are yours and which to hand off.

  • Never dry-sweep, dry-vacuum or blow waste around with a leaf blower. Stirred dust is how people get exposed.
  • Open what you can, walk outside and wait roughly thirty minutes while stale air clears.
  • Hands carry waste to faces, so gloves go on first and a careful wash comes last.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.

Agency pages behind the checklist

Each safety point here traces to published agency guidance, not to anyone selling a service.

  • CDC guidance on rodent dropping cleanup: How to ventilate a shut room, dampen droppings rather than sweep them and get rid of waste without raising dust.
  • EPA listing of rodenticide restrictions: Why some products are limited, with children, pets and wildlife as the driving concern.
