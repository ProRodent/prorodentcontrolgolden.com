Fix the building, not just the room.

Removing animals without closing gaps only invites the next group. Exclusion treats the structure as the problem. The technician walks every wall, vent, roof edge and foundation line and marks anything wider than a quarter inch.

That limit is deliberate. A mouse can squeeze through a gap about as thick as a pencil, so a quarter inch is a safe cutoff for what to close.

Common hiding places for gaps.

  • Spots where utility lines, gas pipes and cable come into the house.
  • Unscreened dryer vents, bath fans and range hoods.
  • Corner of the garage doors and the threshold under them.
  • Soffits, fascia and the points where a roof meets a wall.
  • Foundation vents, crawl space doors and cracked masonry.
  • Decks, steps and porches set against the siding.

Freeze and thaw can nudge a seam open at any of these places. A hairline crack in late summer may be a mouse-sized hole by February, which is why we check after winter as well as at the first visit.

Keeping the seal sound.

  • Look over each repair twice a year, in spring and again in fall.
  • Replace a worn door sweep immediately.
  • Hold plants and wood stacks back from the foundation.
  • Report fresh gnaw marks early, before they widen.

Not sure whether to close a gap yourself? An inspection shows which ones are critical and which can wait.

On your side of the door.

  • Close the door on the noisiest room and leave it shut until the technician comes.
  • Hold off on sweeping or vacuuming pellets, since both fling dry particles upward.
  • Pass on loose bait, because a poisoned animal can crawl into a wall and die there.
  • Jot down the hour of each noise so a pattern shows.

Do and do not, for rodent waste

Hurrying a cleanup can do more harm than the mess. Keep to the order shown.

  • Skip brooms and vacuums entirely. They throw dry droppings into the air you breathe, so they stay in the closet.
  • Give a closed room time to breathe: open doors and windows, then stay out for about half an hour.
  • Hands carry waste to faces, so gloves go on first and a careful wash comes last.
  • Soak the mess through and leave it around five minutes, so the dust cannot rise.
  • Lift a dead rodent into a bag with a gloved hand or a tool, then seal it together with the used towels before the bin.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.

Sealing: your questions answered.

What is wrong with spray foam?

Rodents chew it and then use the shreds to line nests. Foam can fill around metal, but it should never be the only barrier.

Could sealing shut an animal inside?

Not when it follows trapping. We confirm that activity has stopped before closing the main entries.

Does freeze and thaw break a seal?

Movement from temperature swings can open sealant over time, which is one reason to recheck repairs after winter.

How long does the work take?

A small house can be finished in a day. Large or complicated rooflines take longer, and the plan gives an estimate.

Do you repair roofs or siding?

No. We close rodent entries. Bigger roofing or siding work goes to other trades, and we say so when we see it.
