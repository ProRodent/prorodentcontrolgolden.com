Seasons and entry points.

A change of season can move a rodent problem from the yard to the house. A tidy exterior in October is the cheapest protection available.

Warnings inside and out.

Listen and look for the items below. If more than one fits, a visit is worth booking.

  • Soft scampering in the ceiling at dusk.
  • Pellets scattered over a shelf or workbench.
  • Insulation pulled loose or flattened.
  • Birdseed that disappears overnight.
  • A stain on drywall with a sour odor.

Slopes, brush and cover at the edge.

Where a lot meets open ground, cover can reach the foundation. Tall grass, brush and a stack of firewood give rodents a hidden route to the wall, and clearing a few feet of soil beside the house is a cheap fix.

If you keep a vehicle, trailer or camper on the property, check it too. Anything parked for weeks can hold a nest, and the garage is often where animals settle first.

Do and do not, for rodent waste

Hurrying a cleanup can do more harm than the mess. Keep to the order shown.

  • Avoid any method that raises dust, which rules out brooms, shop vacs and compressed air near droppings.
  • Open what you can, walk outside and wait roughly thirty minutes while stale air clears.
  • Hands carry waste to faces, so gloves go on first and a careful wash comes last.
  • Wet everything thoroughly with a household disinfectant or diluted bleach, and let it stand a few minutes before wiping.
  • Take up the wet waste with paper towels, drop them in a plastic bag along with any dead animal, tie it shut and put it in the trash.
  • Fever, aches or shortness of breath within a few weeks of cleaning calls for a doctor. Say what you handled. A pest company cannot advise on health.
